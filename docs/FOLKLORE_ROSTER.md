# Folklore Essence Roster

## Scope and player model

This is a **40-entry design roster** for the ARPG's folklore collection. The entities are unlock inspirations for powers cast by the player's own custom avatar. They are not bosses, basic mobs, party members, summons, or AI supporters. The player remains the combat protagonist and equips exactly two distinct main abilities at once; each unlocked essence offers four ability concepts from which to choose.

The roster is separate from the legacy TCG creature catalog. It is a research and content-design document, not live ARPG card data. No folklore claim below should be read as a claim that the Folklard power or mechanic belongs to the source tradition.

The 40 entries are grouped by the best-supported geographic scope of the cited account. This is not a claim that any country or people has one uniform folklore.

`Rarity` is a game-economy pacing label only. It does not measure the importance, age, legitimacy, or cultural value of a tradition. Unlock routes are design suggestions using internal currency and in-game progression; they do not authorize real-money purchases or specify final lottery odds.

## Player-facing rules

- An owned essence unlocks its four listed ability options. The avatar equips exactly two distinct ability IDs across the full collection.
- The game does not spawn the named figure to follow, fight for, or protect the player. Visual effects may use a brief echo, projectile, wave, root, or other non-character motif; they must not become an autonomous folklore-character companion.
- Do not put these 40 in the basic-mob or boss roster. A separate encounter roster may use other figures only after its own research and review.
- Preserve each cited source's geographic scope and variation. A regional or literary version must not be presented as the universal version.
- Sensitive entries are candidates for culturally informed review before final art, voice, animation, or release. If review advises against a playable treatment, retain the research note and replace the entry rather than forcing it into combat.

## Brazil and South America — 14

### 01. Boitatá

- **Tradition / origin:** Brazilian Indigenous-rooted folklore; the Espírito Santo state education/music series records Tupi-Guarani naming and multiple local variants.
- **Documented account:** A fiery or luminous serpent protects forest and animals, with accounts differing on its form and story. [FAMES, *Boitatá*](https://musicanarede.fames.es.gov.br/Media/MusicaNaRede/Livros/Lendas%20Brasileiras/Boitat%C3%A1.pdf) and [Brazilian National Library Digital, “Folclore Brasileiro | Dia do Saci”](https://bndigital.bn.gov.br/artigos/folclore-brasileiro-dia-do-saci-31-de-outubro/).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A mobile fire-and-light essence whose effects reward pursuit and interrupt attacks. The damage kit is invented for the game.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Fire; chase-and-burn striker; rare; mobile damage dealer.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Rasteira Incandescente** — dash, leaving a short burning trail. B. **Olhar de Brasa** — reveal and mark enemies in a narrow cone. C. **Serpente Orbital** — a brief ring of fire blocks one projectile, then bursts. D. **Fogo que Corre** — a fast curving projectile that scorches its path.
- **Suggested unlock:** Internal-currency lottery pool; also a later direct Archive purchase.

### 02. Curupira

- **Tradition / origin:** Brazilian folklore with Indigenous roots; the cited Brazilian National Library and state education sources describe a forest and fauna protector, while details vary by retelling.
- **Documented account:** Often represented with backward-facing feet and a protective relationship to forests and animals. [Brazilian National Library Digital](https://bndigital.bn.gov.br/artigos/folclore-brasileiro-dia-do-saci-31-de-outubro/) and [FAMES, *Curupira*](https://musicanarede.fames.es.gov.br/Media/MusicaNaRede/Livros/Lendas%20Brasileiras/Curupira.pdf).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A territory-control and misdirection essence. The game does not claim that its traps or dash are traditional powers.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Nature; terrain control and feints; uncommon; controller.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Rastro ao Contrário** — leave a false trail that draws nearby enemies off course. B. **Raízes Ancestrais** — root targets in a small chosen patch. C. **Passo Invertido** — dash, then snap back to the starting point after a short delay. D. **Guarda da Mata** — gain a brief bark-like shield while standing on natural ground.
- **Suggested unlock:** Internal-currency lottery pool; also a later direct Archive purchase.

### 03. Caipora

- **Tradition / origin:** Brazilian popular folklore; the entry is grounded in the state-produced *Lendas Brasileiras* educational volume rather than assigned to a single Indigenous people.
- **Documented account:** The cited educational retelling glosses Tupi *caapora* as “forest dweller” and presents the figure as a protector of animals from sport hunters; it notes regional differences, including accounts connecting the figure with the Amazon or Pantanal and differing relations to Curupira. This is one retelling, not a universal Indigenous account. [FAMES, *Caipora*](https://musicanarede.fames.es.gov.br/Media/MusicaNaRede/Livros/Lendas%20Brasileiras/Caipora.pdf).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A mobile hunter's toolkit built around marks and player-placed snares; it is not a companion or a hunting animal.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Nature; trapper and skirmisher; common; setup and pursuit.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Marca de Caça** — mark one enemy so the next hit briefly slows it. B. **Laço de Cipó** — place a tripwire that roots the first enemy crossing it. C. **Salto do Ramo** — hop over a target or low obstacle. D. **Flecha de Folhas** — fire a light projectile that ricochets once from terrain.
- **Suggested unlock:** Direct purchase with in-game currency.

### 04. Saci-Pererê

- **Tradition / origin:** Brazilian folklore shaped by several cultural inheritances; the National Library article describes regional change and African and Indigenous influences rather than one fixed origin story.
- **Documented account:** A one-legged, agile trickster is a common modern depiction; the Library notes that the figure's name and stories have changed by region. [Brazilian National Library Digital](https://bndigital.bn.gov.br/artigos/folclore-brasileiro-dia-do-saci-31-de-outubro/) and [Goiânia Municipal Education Department, “Folclore”](https://sme.goiania.go.gov.br/conexaoescola/ensino_fundamental/folclore/).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A wind, feint, and projectile-redirection essence. Do not reproduce demeaning racial caricatures; the player's avatar is not transformed into Saci.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Storm; feint and disruption; common; evasive controller.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Redemoinho Travesso** — spin up a small wind ring that pushes enemies away. B. **Assobio de Desvio** — turn one incoming projectile toward a nearby target. C. **Pulo de Fumaça** — evade in a burst, leaving an afterimage. D. **Garrafa de Vendaval** — briefly store a projectile in a wind pocket and release it in the aimed direction.
- **Suggested unlock:** Direct purchase with in-game currency.

### 05. Iara / Uiara

- **Tradition / origin:** Brazilian river folklore; the cited public sources connect popular Iara stories with Tupi-Guarani cultural histories but also document only particular versions.
- **Documented account:** A river-associated figure often represented in later retellings as a siren whose voice draws listeners. [Brazilian National Library Digital](https://bndigital.bn.gov.br/artigos/folclore-brasileiro-dia-do-saci-31-de-outubro/) and [Goiânia Municipal Education Department](https://sme.goiania.go.gov.br/conexaoescola/ensino_fundamental/folclore/).
- **Folklard adaptation:** Water control, sound interruption, and self-sustain. No power uses coercive seduction, and no account is presented as universal.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Water; sound controller and sustain; epic; control with recovery.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Canto de Correnteza** — a sound wave interrupts and slows enemies in a cone. B. **Espelho d'Água** — place a pool and dash to it once before it fades. C. **Maré de Retorno** — pull enemies toward the pool's edge, without requiring damage. D. **Fonte Serena** — restore a small amount of the avatar's health over time.
- **Suggested unlock:** Internal-currency lottery pool; alternate quest route after a river-region challenge.
- **Review note:** Keep the focus on water and sound; avoid romanticized pursuit or sexualized imagery.

### 06. Boto-cor-de-rosa

- **Tradition / origin:** Amazonian Brazilian river narratives; the National Library account places the story in the regional festival context and describes a human disguise in one recorded version.
- **Documented account:** In the cited telling, a pink river dolphin becomes a white-clad young man during festivities. Versions carry gendered and coercive themes. [Brazilian National Library Digital](https://bndigital.bn.gov.br/artigos/folclore-brasileiro-dia-do-saci-31-de-outubro/) and [FAMES, *Boto Cor-de-Rosa*](https://musicanarede.fames.es.gov.br/Media/MusicaNaRede/Livros/Lendas%20Brasileiras/Boto%20Cor-de-Rosa.pdf).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A river-mobility and illusion kit centered on the avatar's own decoy and escape tools. It intentionally omits seduction and coercion.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Water; mobility and illusion; uncommon; evasive skirmisher.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Véu da Festa** — create a brief visual decoy at the avatar's former position. B. **Passagem pelo Rio** — dash through enemies and become briefly untargetable. C. **Rastro de Espuma** — lay a water path that speeds the avatar and slows pursuers. D. **Chapéu de Bruma** — create a small fog field that obscures the avatar from ranged aim.
- **Suggested unlock:** Direct purchase with in-game currency.
- **Review note:** The source story includes coercive gender dynamics; none are used as a player reward or mechanic.

### 07. Cuca

- **Tradition / origin:** Brazilian popular folklore recorded in the FAMES state educational *Lendas Brasileiras* series. The source is a retelling, not evidence of one pan-Brazilian original.
- **Documented account:** In the cited FAMES retelling, Cuca is an old witch with a crocodile-like body, invoked in a bedtime warning to frighten children. That specific retelling is not a universal account. [FAMES, *Cuca*](https://musicanarede.fames.es.gov.br/Media/MusicaNaRede/Livros/Lendas%20Brasileiras/Cuca.pdf).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A spell-and-sleep control set; the game invents every combat effect below and does not use child abduction or harm as a mechanic.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Spirit; sleep, fear, and debuff; rare; controller.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Sussurro Sonolento** — briefly daze common enemies in a small area. B. **Riso de Pesadelo** — fear pulse that makes affected enemies retreat from the avatar. C. **Marca de Feitiço** — curse one target, weakening its next attack. D. **Caldeirão de Eco** — absorb one minor projectile and return it as a slow orb.
- **Suggested unlock:** Internal-currency lottery pool; also a later direct Archive purchase.

### 08. Mula sem Cabeça

- **Tradition / origin:** Brazilian legend in the FAMES educational series; retellings are regional and should not be collapsed into a single religious or moral lesson.
- **Documented account:** The cited Goiânia education page describes a cursed, headless quadruped that runs while emitting fire; it records one common version, not a universal origin. [Goiânia Municipal Education Department](https://sme.goiania.go.gov.br/conexaoescola/ensino_fundamental/folclore/) and [FAMES, *Mula sem Cabeça*](https://musicanarede.fames.es.gov.br/Media/MusicaNaRede/Livros/Lendas%20Brasileiras/Mula%20sem%20Cabe%C3%A7a.pdf).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A fire-charged movement kit. It does not shame a real person or enact punishment for sexuality or religious conduct.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Fire; charge and area denial; uncommon; vanguard.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Galope de Brasa** — charge down a marked line after a visible warning. B. **Pegadas Ardentes** — leave short-lived fire patches while moving. C. **Estouro Noturno** — radial stomp that pushes enemies back. D. **Freio de Cinzas** — parry a close strike, then release a small flame burst.
- **Suggested unlock:** Direct purchase with in-game currency.

### 09. Matinta Pereira / Matinta Perera

- **Tradition / origin:** Amazonian oral narrative, documented here in the specific Pará communities of Curuçambaba (Bujaru) and Taperaçu Campo (Bragança), not as one universal Amazonian story.
- **Documented account:** Field studies record a shape-changing figure, commonly gendered female in those accounts, and describe offerings and local narrative variation. [Federal University of Pará repository, Curuçambaba study](https://repositorio.ufpa.br/jspui/handle/2011/10378) and [UFPA *Amazônica* anthropology journal, Taperaçu Campo study](https://periodicos.ufpa.br/index.php/amazonica/article/view/1878/0).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A sound, reveal, and interrupt essence. It avoids making age, gender, or poverty a monster trait.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Spirit; sound utility and interruption; common; anti-caster controller.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Assobio Revelador** — reveal hidden enemies and traps in a cone. B. **Chamado Distante** — interrupt an enemy wind-up from medium range. C. **Voo de Sombra** — short directional evade that crosses low obstacles. D. **Silêncio da Mata** — mute enemy casting in a small area for a brief window.
- **Suggested unlock:** Direct purchase with in-game currency; alternate exploration discovery in the Amazon region.
- **Review note:** Field sources discuss gender, old age, poverty, and community belief; avoid making those people targets or jokes.

### 10. Cobra Grande / Boiúna

- **Tradition / origin:** Amazonian Brazilian river narratives, with related but non-identical accounts across communities and places.
- **Documented account:** A large water serpent is recorded in Brazilian folklore sources as an owner or presence of river waters; the cited scholarship treats regional versions rather than a single standardized creature. [USP thesis, “No rastro da cobra-grande: variações míticas e sociocosmológicas”](https://teses.usp.br/teses/disponiveis/8/8134/tde-05122007-151505/pt-br.html) and [Revista Brasileira de História / SciELO, “The Negros d’Água of the Ribeira”](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbh/a/xGpNzrctFTmZywzTZjy4zPc/?format=pdf&lang=en).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A player-directed water-and-terrain shaper. The avatar creates currents; the serpent is never spawned as a follower.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Water; terrain manipulation and pull; epic; area controller.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Remanso de Arrasto** — pull enemies toward a chosen eddy. B. **Margem Erguida** — raise a short water barrier that blocks projectiles. C. **Canal Profundo** — create a fast lane that carries the avatar forward. D. **Laço de Maré** — send a broad curved wave that briefly roots targets at its edge.
- **Suggested unlock:** Progression reward for exploring an Amazon river dungeon.

### 11. Mapinguari

- **Tradition / origin:** Pan-Amazonian folklore studied through region-specific narratives; the term and descriptions have changed through intercommunity retellings.
- **Documented account:** Scholarship cautions against treating every Mapinguari account as one fixed animal or using cryptozoological speculation as folklore fact. [Waldemar Ferreira Netto, USP catalogue record for *O monstruoso mapinguari pan-amazônico*](https://repositorio.usp.br/item/002786636) and [J. Galeano, *Folktales of the Amazon* excerpts](https://myweb.fsu.edu/jgaleano/pdf/FolktalesExcerpts_sm.pdf).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A heavy, defensive forest essence. The game does not assert an extinct-animal explanation or a universal body design.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Nature; armor, stagger, and shockwave; legendary; durable striker.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Couro de Casca** — gain a brief damage-reducing guard. B. **Rugido Oco** — interrupt enemies in a frontal cone. C. **Passo de Tronco** — stomp, sending a low shockwave through the ground. D. **Raiz de Retorno** — place a root mark and pull the avatar back to it once.
- **Suggested unlock:** Progression reward from an exploration achievement, not a boss drop.
- **Review note:** Confirm the specific regional narrative before selecting silhouette or sound design.

### 12. Vitória-Régia

- **Tradition / origin:** Brazilian retelling about the Amazonian water lily. The cited source is a Fundação Joaquim Nabuco educational research page and one published version, not a claim of a single pan-Indigenous tradition.
- **Documented account:** One version links the flower's night bloom to a young woman and the moon. [Fundação Joaquim Nabuco, “Vitória-Régia”](https://pesquisaescolar.fundaj.gov.br/pt-br/artigo/vitoria-regia/).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A floral water-ward and recovery essence. The game invents the healing and defense mechanics.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Water; healing, shield, and reflection; uncommon; defensive sustain.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Folha Flutuante** — glide a short distance over hazards. B. **Flor de Luar** — restore health gradually while standing in a small pool. C. **Pétala-Guarda** — form a brief shield of overlapping petals. D. **Bacia Refletora** — reflect one projectile from a placed water surface.
- **Suggested unlock:** Direct purchase with in-game currency; alternate quest reward in a night-garden area.
- **Review note:** Keep the source's version label visible in lore and do not invent an Indigenous group's authorship.

### 13. Alicanto

- **Tradition / origin:** Northern Chilean folklore, especially Atacama and Coquimbo as recorded by the *Enciclopedia Chilena*.
- **Documented account:** The encyclopedia describes a nocturnal, metallic-feathered bird associated with gold and silver deposits; stories warn that its light can lead followers astray. [Biblioteca del Congreso Nacional de Chile, *Enciclopedia Chilena*, “Alicanto” entry](https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Enciclopedia_Chilena/Folclore/Las_Creencias).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A treasure-sense and glint-control essence, not a promise that the source story is a mining guide.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Storm; reveal, decoy, and ranged utility; rare; scout and precision striker.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Asa de Prata** — fire a returning crescent projectile. B. **Rastro Mineral** — reveal nearby chests and breakables through walls for a short time. C. **Brilho Falso** — place a decoy glint that draws enemy aim. D. **Mergulho Metálico** — dive through a marked line and scatter a brief burst of sparks.
- **Suggested unlock:** Internal-currency lottery pool; also an exploration challenge in a Chile-inspired high-desert region.

### 14. Camahueto

- **Tradition / origin:** Chilote folklore from Chiloé, with recorded reach into nearby Llanquihue; do not label it a generic Chilean or pan-Mapuche being.
- **Documented account:** The *Enciclopedia Chilena* records a powerful aquatic calf with one or two horn descriptions and river-to-sea variants. [Biblioteca del Congreso Nacional de Chile, *Enciclopedia Chilena*, “Camahuete, El”](https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Enciclopedia_Chilena/Folclore/Camahuete%2C_El) and [Memoria Chilena, “Religiosidad, mito e identidad chilota”](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-3672.html).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A braced water vanguard using the horn as an invented visual motif. The power kit is not traditional.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Water; charge, stagger, and guard; epic; vanguard.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Chifre Dourado** — charge in a straight line after a visible tell. B. **Corte de Estuário** — dash and leave a shallow stream that slows pursuers. C. **Casco de Maré** — brace to reduce damage and resist knockback. D. **Rompente de Cascos** — radial stomp that breaks enemy guard.
- **Suggested unlock:** Progression challenge tied to a coastal exploration route.

## Central America, Caribbean, and North America — 6

### 15. Ahuízotl

- **Tradition / origin:** Mexica/Nahua source in central Mexico; the entry is grounded in Book 11 of the *Florentine Codex*, a colonial-era work co-authored by Nahua elders, authors, and artists with Bernardino de Sahagún.
- **Documented account:** Folio 70v describes an unusual aquatic animal living at springs and water outlets. [Getty Research Institute, Digital Florentine Codex, Book 11, folio 70v](https://florentinecodex.getty.edu/es/book/11/folio/70v); [Getty, Digital Florentine Codex overview](https://florentinecodex.getty.edu/en/).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A spring-water controller based on the source's water habitat; no claim is made that the Codex's description maps cleanly to modern monster art.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Water; pull, trap, and counter; legendary; controller.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Mão da Nascente** — pull one target a short distance. B. **Poço Súbito** — create a brief spring trap under a marked enemy. C. **Bolha Parada** — hold one projectile in a bubble, then release it. D. **Corrente Oculta** — dash through an enemy and emerge behind it.
- **Suggested unlock:** Progression reward for a codex/library exploration quest; not a boss identity.
- **Review note:** Keep Nahua naming and source context visible; do not lift Codex imagery without rights and cultural review.

### 16. La Llorona

- **Tradition / origin:** Mexican and wider Hispanic American oral tradition with many regional versions. The Handbook of Texas discusses Texas and Mexican lore; it does not establish one origin story.
- **Documented account:** A recurring version is a woman heard weeping near water, with the story serving different community and social functions. [Texas State Historical Association, “La Llorona”](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/la-llorona); [Domino Renee Perez, *There Was a Woman: La Llorona from Folklore to Popular Culture*](https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9780292718111).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A grief-and-echo sound-control essence. Child death, family violence, and colonial interpretations are not used as collectible spectacle.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Spirit; area slow and interrupt; epic; zone controller.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Lamento de Margem** — send a crescent sound wave that interrupts. B. **Rio de Ecos** — slow enemies in a marked circle. C. **Eco Distante** — repeat the avatar's last short dash as a harmless echo. D. **Vigília Silenciosa** — gain a brief ward after remaining still through an enemy wind-up.
- **Suggested unlock:** Internal-currency lottery pool; alternate quest route through a river-story archive.
- **Review note:** Keep the mechanics focused on sound and remembrance, never child harm.

### 17. Cadejo

- **Tradition / origin:** Central American oral legend, documented across Guatemala and neighboring regions; black/white roles and details vary. No pre-Columbian origin is asserted here.
- **Documented account:** The cited folklore reader and archive describe a supernatural dog, with black/white variants whose roles and details vary; these collections are not an exhaustive map of living accounts. [University of San Carlos de Guatemala, CECEG, *Three Studies on Folklore*](https://ceceg.usac.edu.gt/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/8.-THREE-STUDIES-ON-FOLKLORE.pdf); [USC Digital Folklore Archives, “El Cadejo”](https://folklore.usc.edu/el-cadejo/).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A crossroads and shadow-step essence. Black/white imagery is not treated as a universal moral binary.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Spirit; feint, parry, and route control; common; evasive defender.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Passo da Encruzilhada** — choose one of two short dash exits. B. **Pegadas Duplicadas** — leave two false trails that split enemy aim. C. **Latido Guardião** — cancel one close-range hit with a timed bark-like pulse. D. **Sombra que Acompanha** — cloak briefly after crossing a marked threshold.
- **Suggested unlock:** Direct purchase with in-game currency.

### 18. Chupacabra

- **Tradition / origin:** A modern Puerto Rican contemporary legend first reported in the 1990s, later developing multiple forms across the Americas. It is not an ancient Indigenous being.
- **Documented account:** A history article documents the Puerto Rican reports and later cross-border meanings; reports and descriptions varied over time. [CSU East Bay, *Historia*, “El Chupacabra: Embodiment of Fears from Both Sides of the Border”](https://www.csueastbay.edu/history/files/docs/2020-east-bay-historia-vol-4.pdf); [BYU, “American Cryptids” folklore material](https://contentdm.lib.byu.edu/digital/api/collection/folklore/id/1616/download).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A fictional nocturnal ambusher with mark-and-leech mechanics. The design does not portray real animal attacks as supernatural fact.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Spirit; mark, dash, and limited life-steal; common; isolated-target skirmisher.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Pulo Noturno** — pounce to a visible point, with a short recovery window. B. **Marca de Presa** — mark an isolated enemy to improve the avatar's next hit. C. **Farpas da Sombra** — fire a short spread of spectral quills. D. **Fôlego Roubado** — recover a small amount of health only after hitting a marked target.
- **Suggested unlock:** Direct purchase with in-game currency.

### 19. Jackalope

- **Documented account:** The Smithsonian traces the American jackalope's modern popularity to taxidermy hoaxes and discusses older horned-hare imagery separately. [Smithsonian Institution, “The World’s Scariest Rabbit”](https://www.si.edu/stories/worlds-scariest-rabbit).
- **Folklard adaptation:** A light ricochet-and-mobility essence with no claim of ancient origin.
- **Element / archetype / rarity / role:** Nature; ricochet, dash, and feint; common; nimble ranged skirmisher.
- **Four power concepts:** A. **Ricochete de Galhada** — bounce a light projectile once. B. **Salto da Pradaria** — vault through a short line of enemies. C. **Chifre de Amora** — charge and stagger the first target hit. D. **Rastro de Acampamento** — place false tracks that misdirect enemy pursuit.
- **Suggested unlock:** Direct purchase with in-game currency.

C. **Trilha de Bruma** — become briefly untargetable while dashing. D. **Olhos na Clareira** — reveal enemy trails and hidden switches for a short duration.

## Europe — 6

### 21. Kelpie

- **Tradition / origin:** Scottish water-horse tradition. Scots-language dictionary records include river and ford accounts; distinctions from other Gaelic water horses vary among sources.
- **Documented account:** A kelpie is recorded as a water being, often horse-shaped and associated with dangerous water crossings. ## Research method and limits

1. Prefer a primary or community-specific source where available: the Digital Florentine Codex for Ahuízotl; fieldwork and regional oral-narrative studies for Matinta Pereira; local government, national-library, and museum sources for place-bound traditions; and academic studies for literary or historical traditions.
2. Use institutional educational retellings as evidence that a version is documented, not as proof that it is ancient, universal, or owned by one people. This limitation is called out for Cuca, Caipora, Vitória-Régia, and several Brazilian regional stories.
3. For contemporary legends such as Chupacabra, identify them as modern. For commercial or settler-made characters such as Jackalope, identify their modern media history. Do not invent precolonial origins.
4. For each entry, the “Documented account” is a short paraphrase of the linked source. The “Folklard adaptation,” ability names, mechanics, rarity, role, and unlock route are game inventions.
5. The citations are a research baseline, not cultural authorization. Entries tied to living traditions or histories of colonial violence require expert/community review before their final design is approved.
